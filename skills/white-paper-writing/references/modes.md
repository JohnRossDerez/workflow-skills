# Outcome modes

Select the main reader outcome. Mode changes inquiry, synthesis, and acceptance criteria; it does not relax evidence standards. A recommendation can contain an explainer without becoming a hybrid configuration.

## investigate

Use for open technical or research questions, system assessments, competing explanations, and finding out whether a proposition holds. Start with questions and a provisional explanation map. Search for observations that discriminate between explanations, including evidence that weakens the initial framing. Avoid writing a confident candidate answer before inspecting the decisive inputs.

Synthesize what is established, contested, absent, and sensitive to assumptions. Structure around findings and their implications. An inconclusive or negative answer is a valid result. Acceptance requires coverage of credible alternatives, an explicit stopping rationale, and a conclusion no stronger than the evidence.

## decide

Use for architecture, methodology, implementation, tooling, operating choices, and technical prioritization. Define the decision owner, feasible alternatives, baseline/no-action option, criteria, constraints, horizon, and reversible versus irreversible consequences. Elicit missing preferences only if they can change the ranking; otherwise state the assumption.

Compare alternatives on the same basis. Distinguish evidence about outcomes from the owner's preference weights. Test which uncertain inputs could reverse the recommendation. Structure around the recommendation, comparison, decisive tradeoffs, sensitivity, and implementation conditions. Acceptance requires a justified choice and conditions under which a different choice becomes preferable.

## explain

Use for technical explainers, system descriptions, primers, methodology notes, and subject overviews. Map the audience's prior knowledge, essential concepts, common confusions, mechanisms, and boundaries. A useful example need not establish a general empirical claim; label hypothetical examples.

Research for conceptual coverage and correctness. Structure from the reader's existing understanding to new concepts, mechanisms, and applications. Do not force every explainer into a commercial thesis or recommendation. Acceptance requires accurate simplification, useful examples, defined terms, and no consequential omission or misleading analogy.

## argue

Use for internal technical positions such as an architecture rationale, methodology defense, design proposal, or standards interpretation. Record the proposed position, likely reviewer objections, permitted restructuring, protected headings, disclosure boundaries, and points the user considers essential. Preserve those choices unless evidence requires a factual correction.

Map each premise to support, qualifications, and the strongest material objection. Evidence may justify a narrower position. State internal assertions as such and scenarios as scenarios; authorization to circulate never becomes evidence for empirical validity. Do not turn selective research into a claim of exhaustive coverage.

Structure the strongest supportable case in the requested hierarchy. Address counterevidence where it changes the reader's interpretation. Acceptance requires fidelity to the commission, passage-level support for factual premises, visible attribution, and honest treatment of consequential objections. If the central proposition cannot be supported, explain that specifically and provide a reviewable alternative within the authorized scope.
