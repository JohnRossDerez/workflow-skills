## Message recovered

The white-paper-writing skill is a way for an agent to turn research into a technical argument that engineers and reviewers can inspect. It asks the author to plan what the reader needs in order, register support for consequential claims, write one canonical Markdown manuscript, edit passages in context, build requested formats, and review the actual deliverables. The records expose where a claim came from and whether a review is stale; the author and reviewers still have to decide whether the inference, prose, and document are good. The paper recommends compact planning for an ordinary internal R&D paper and the fuller dependency representation when coordination or repeated impact queries warrant its maintenance.

## Mechanism understood

The compact route begins with a reader brief and a document plan. An evidence ledger distinguishes sources, claims, and links from passages to support, with number and figure records when needed. Its example is useful: selected test passes support a statement about those tests, not production reliability. An author then makes located prose edits, documenting representative before/after passages and checking that changes preserve the sourced meaning. Build helpers resolve registered numbers and citations from the canonical manuscript, stage requested formats, and fingerprint inputs and outputs. Evidence, editorial, fresh-reader, rendered-page, and structural checks each address different failure modes. A changed source or output invalidates a prior review through hashes, although a hash cannot validate the reviewer's reasoning.

I take the fuller workflow to add a maintained graph of dependencies and coordination records. I understand that technical length and multiple formats alone do not trigger it. The description of how to choose and query that graph is intentionally high level; it gives me the decision criterion, not an operational walkthrough.
