# Human writing standard

For compact projects, use the document plan in place of a tree/node contract. A separate voice profile is optional when samples or a maintained house style justify it; the reader relationship can stay in the brief. The prose and semantic standards below remain shared.

Graph compilation makes the reasoning inspectable; it does not by itself make the brief readable or recognizably authored. Apply this standard after a node is supported and again after the brief is assembled. The goal is natural, reader-centered executive writing, not imitation for authorship concealment or detector evasion.

## Governing principles

1. **Meet the reader where they are.** Choose vocabulary, context, tone, and technical depth from the defined audience and purpose.
2. **Build understanding progressively.** Move from given information to new information; introduce nontrivial concepts before relying on them and make headings and transitions honest promises about what follows.
3. **Make reasoning visible.** Connect evidence, mechanism, inference, qualification, and implication closely enough that the reader can reconstruct the synthesis.
4. **Keep meaning stable.** Preserve definitions, scope, attribution, uncertainty, and material qualifications through compression, revision, and voice adaptation.
5. **Allocate attention deliberately.** Spend words and visual structure according to what the reader must understand, decide, challenge, or do.

## Establish the voice

Record the reader relationship and current tone in `planning/brief.md`. Put a reusable evidence-based profile in `planning/voice.md`: provenance, confidence, voice fingerprint, lexical and syntactic choices, rhetoric, structure, mechanics, tone modulation, negative space, sample defects not to imitate, calibration transformations, and a compact runtime card. Treat supplied samples as evidence of preferences, not permission to reproduce distinctive passages. Do not invent personal experience, quotations, anecdotes, opinions, or an institutional voice. With one sample, label the profile provisional.

Use language the executive audience already uses. Introduce an unfamiliar term before substantive use, then translate technical detail into consequence, choice, timing, exposure, or confidence without hiding the mechanism that makes the conclusion true. Preserve a real author's characteristic choices when samples exist, while correcting ambiguity and error. Without samples, use direct, restrained professional prose rather than a generic corporate persona.

## Write developed prose

- State the paragraph's point or consequence early, then supply the explanation, evidence, implication, or qualification needed to trust it. Do not turn every paragraph into a slogan or identical template.
- Prefer concrete subjects and precise verbs. Name the owner of an action when ownership matters; use passive voice deliberately when the actor is unknown or immaterial.
- Keep qualifications close to the claims they limit. Compression must never increase certainty, causality, or generality beyond the evidence.
- Make transitions express the actual logical relationship. Remove canned transitions, throat-clearing, and repeated mini-conclusions.
- Let paragraph endings create a specific expectation, consequence, contrast, or unresolved question that the next paragraph fulfills. Write joins after assembly when they depend on distant context.
- Vary sentence and paragraph length because the meaning changes, not by randomizing syntax. Mix concise decisions with enough developed explanation to avoid choppy memo fragments.
- Use examples, observations, and local detail drawn from the evidence. Never add decorative specificity that was not established.
- Synthesize source ideas in independent language and structure. Use exact source wording only for a necessary, marked quotation; citation does not make close paraphrase acceptable.
- Repeat a detail only to aid recall after meaningful distance, reinterpret it, use it in a decision, or close a deliberate thread. Otherwise treat it as established context.
- Prefer prose for a developed line of thought. Use bullets, tables, headings, or callouts only when the material is genuinely parallel, sequential, or easier to compare in that form.

## Resist model-default prose

Revise passages that are uniformly polished but semantically thin. Look especially for repeated sentence frames, excessive symmetry, long abstract noun chains, inflated adjectives, needless restatement, mechanical three-item lists, rhetorical questions used as transitions, and contrast formulas such as “not X, but Y.” Remove stock openings and claims of importance that the content does not demonstrate. A local ban list may help catch recurring habits, but do not reduce style review to a list of forbidden words.

Human-like writing does not require deliberate errors, slang, contractions, emotional language, or uneven quality. It requires situated judgment: the right detail, cadence, vocabulary, and degree of explanation for this reader and purpose.

## Located prose-editing pass

After assembly, read a paragraph in its surrounding argument before editing individual sentences. Keep one reader relationship and let local emphasis follow the work: an observation can be direct, a mechanism may need a developed sentence, and an inference carries its qualification. These are diagnostic functions, not labels to store for every sentence. Do not prescribe a tone change between adjacent sentences or vary certainty for effect.

Locate the actual reading burden: a delayed verb, several new concepts at once, nested clauses, stacked abstractions, repeated setup, an unexplained connection or a qualification detached from its claim. Explain what the reader must hold in memory and edit the affected passage. Split unrelated propositions; keep a longer sentence when the relation between its clauses is the point. Preserve useful domain terms. A short sentence is not automatically clear, and a recurring construction may help readers compare genuinely parallel facts.

Use a suitable supplied or already available exemplar when it clarifies the audience's preferred depth or rhythm; otherwise edit directly. Borrow an editorial strategy, not its facts or distinctive wording. Do not research exemplars solely to satisfy this pass, invent a persona or add a sentence graph. Sentence-length counts and repeated openings can locate passages; neither those counts nor detector scores are quality targets.

In the existing editorial notes, retain representative located diagnoses, the before/after passage and the reason for each substantive edit. A small clean draft may need only a short account of the inspected passages. Check revised claims against their sources: preserve scope, attribution, causal strength, uncertainty and numerical meaning, and update affected evidence records and reviews when meaning changes. Stop when the located reading problem is resolved; avoid repeated polishing without a reader-relevant reason.

For an executive brief, make the consequence or choice recoverable early, then supply enough mechanism to justify it. Move optional specialist detail to a reference or appendix when useful; keep decision-changing caveats in the main path.

For an important revision, test the actual artifact with a fresh reader. Ask what they understood and where reading became difficult before offering an author's diagnosis. If comparing versions, use the same audience commission, conceal condition labels and vary presentation order. Check factual fidelity and retained qualifications before considering clarity or preference. Record located examples and disclose agent-only judgments; one pair is a local appraisal, not evidence of average improvement or human preference.

## Revise in distinct passes

1. **Meaning:** verify the paragraph's point, support, implication, and qualification against the plan or, in a full project, its tree node.
2. **Development:** check that each sentence advances the point and gives the executive enough explanation to evaluate the conclusion.
3. **Voice and diction:** replace generic or inflated language with concrete consequence and action language; preserve useful authorial texture.
4. **Rhythm and continuity:** read in sequence and, when practical, aloud. Repair monotonous cadence, abrupt joins, excessive parenthesis, and accidental repetition without changing meaning.
5. **Copyedit:** correct grammar, spelling, punctuation, and house style only after semantic editing is stable.

For important briefs, test the first page and one detail section early with an intended reader. Ask the reader to paraphrase the answer, requested action, reasons, and material caveats. Revise and retest when misunderstanding would be material. This formative check complements the separately recorded cold-reader review required when reader testing is configured. Readability scores and counts can flag passages for review, but they cannot establish clarity, voice, or quality.

## Research basis

This standard follows current OpenAI prompting guidance to specify prose structure and style, state the main point early, use familiar words and precise verbs, limit formatting, and calibrate technical depth to the reader. U.S. Digital.gov plain-language guidance likewise makes the audience primary, recommends upfront organization and strong verbs, and calls for iterative comprehension testing. Recent ACL studies find that model-modified or model-generated text can favor longer or more complex wording, lower lexical diversity, simpler recurring style profiles, and weaker stylistic accommodation than human writing. Those findings motivate review for homogenization; they are diagnostic tendencies, not a recipe for mechanically manipulating sentence statistics.

- [Digital.gov plain-language principles](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language/principles)
- [Digital.gov writing for understanding](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language/writing)
- [Digital.gov comprehension testing](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language/test)
- [Linguistic and embedding-based profiling of human and model text](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1163/)
- [Stylistic alignment in human and LLM communication](https://aclanthology.org/2025.sigdial-1.16/)

The located editing pass is a practical design choice, not an established universal intervention. Corpus studies describe tendencies; model-edited scientific passages can also improve clarity in selected reader tests. Exemplar-guided adaptation has promising task-specific results but does not establish a benefit from arbitrary tone changes.
